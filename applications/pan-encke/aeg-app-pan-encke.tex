\begin{abstract}
The scalloped edges of Saturn's Encke gap are the gravitational signature of the
shepherd moon Pan.  We give a two-layer account.  The \emph{arithmetic layer}
reads Pan's Lindblad resonance ladder $x=m/(m\pm1)$ as the Farey neighbourhood of
corotation ($x=1$), realized as Ford circles piling up on the hyperbolic upper
half-plane --- a concrete physical instance of the rational-approximation
structure at the core of the HTWR framework.  The \emph{dynamical layer} then
resolves the observed wave: it is neither a propagating density wave nor a (cold
or pressure) edge mode, but Pan's \emph{static tidal distortion} (a forced
epicycle), with radial amplitude $GM_P/(\kappa^2 d^2)\approx0.79$\,km and
azimuthal scale $\approx250$\,km, matching the values extracted from a Cassini
image ($\approx0.65$\,km, $\approx300$\,km).  The two layers are complementary:
the arithmetic skeleton locates the gap (resonance overlap), while the tidal
distortion shapes its edge.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} planetary rings; Encke gap; Pan; Lindblad resonances;
Farey sequence; Ford circles; epicyclic theory; Chirikov resonance overlap.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Pan (mass $M_P\approx4.95\times10^{15}$\,kg, semi-major axis
$a_P=133{,}584$\,km) orbits inside the Encke gap, a $325$-km-wide division in
Saturn's A ring.  Its gravity excites a scalloped (``wavy'') pattern on both gap
edges, observed by Voyager and Cassini and ubiquitous over $360^\circ$ of
longitude \cite{Tiscareno2005WavyEdges}.  The aim of this note is twofold:
(i)~to exhibit the \emph{arithmetic structure} of Pan's resonances --- a Farey
ladder accumulating at corotation --- as a concrete instance of the HTWR
framework's rational-approximation geometry; and (ii)~to resolve the
\emph{dynamical mechanism} of the observed edge wave by elimination, ending at
Pan's static tidal distortion.  The two parts are complementary, not the same
phenomenon: the resonance ladder explains \emph{why the gap is there}, the tidal
distortion explains \emph{why its edge is scalloped}.

\section{The resonance ladder is a Farey sequence (the arithmetic layer)}
\label{sec:farey}

In the linear epicyclic theory, the $m$-th gravitational component of Pan
(pattern speed $\Omega_P$) excites a ring particle at the Lindblad resonance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lindblad}
m(\Omega-\Omega_P)=\pm\kappa,\qquad
\Omega=\kappa=\Omega_P\,(r/a_P)^{-3/2}.
\end{equation}
Writing $x:=\Omega/\Omega_P$, this is $m(x-1)=\pm x$, i.e.\
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ladder}
\text{inner }(r<a_P):\ x=\frac{m}{m-1},\qquad
\text{outer }(r>a_P):\ x=\frac{m}{m+1},\qquad
\frac{r}{a_P}=x^{-2/3}.
\end{equation}
Hence the resonance \emph{frequencies} are exactly the Farey neighbours of
corotation, $x=m/(m\pm1)\to1$ as $m\to\infty$.

\section{Gap opening: the Chirikov resonance-overlap criterion}\label{sec:chirikov}

The gap edge is where the high-$m$ resonances \emph{overlap}.  The relevant
scales are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scales}
R_H=a_P(\mu/3)^{1/3}\approx19.0\text{ km}
\quad(\mu=M_P/M_S=8.7\times10^{-12}),\qquad
\delta r\approx a_P\sqrt\mu\approx0.39\text{ km},
\end{equation}
and the resonance spacing $dR_m=2a_P/(3m^2)=0.293$\,km at $m=551$.  Overlap
(Chirikov) requires $\delta r\gtrsim dR_m$, i.e.\
$m\gtrsim\sqrt{2/(3\sqrt\mu)}\approx475$.  The observed edge ($m\approx551$)
agrees with this to the $\mathcal O(1)$ coefficient of the resonance width.
Since $\mathrm{gap}/R_H\approx8.5$, the gap is opened by the \emph{resonance
forest} (the dense high-$m$ ladder), not by direct Hill-sphere clearing.

\section{The density-wave dispersion (the free wave, eliminated)}
\label{sec:dispersion}

The tight-winding (WKB) dispersion for a self-gravitating, pressure-supported
disk is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dispersion}
(\omega-m\Omega)^2=\kappa^2-2\pi G\Sigma|k|+c^2k^2,
\end{equation}
a closed-form quadratic in the radial wavenumber $k$.  At resonance the radial
wavelength is $\lambda_0=c^2/(G\Sigma)\approx0.75$\,km
($\Sigma=500$\,kg/m$^2$, $c=0.5$\,cm/s), \emph{decreasing} away from resonance
(the ``wrapped-up'' spiral).  This matches the observed ``wavelength varies
smoothly'' signature qualitatively, but the free wave lives at $m<551$
(resonance in the ring); the measured edge wave is $m\approx2800$, whose
resonance lies \emph{inside} the cleared gap.  Hence the observed edge wave is
not the free density wave.

\section{Edge-mode elimination (cold and pressure)}\label{sec:edgemode}

We test the Papaloizou--Lin edge mode in the cold WKB limit and with pressure.

\emph{Cold WKB} ($\sigma^2=\kappa^2-2\pi G\Sigma|k|$, reflecting edge, Airy
quantization $\int|k|\dd r=(n+3/4)\pi$): the turning point
$r_L=a_P((m+1)/m)^{2/3}$ lies outside the edge only for $m<551$; for
$m\ge551$ the resonance is inside the gap and the edge is far off-resonance
(evanescent decay length $\approx0.5$\,m).

\emph{Pressure} ($Q=c\kappa/(\pi G\Sigma)\approx6$): the existence condition is
geometric ($m<548$), independent of $c$; at $m=2800$ the pressure only yields a
short wave of radial wavelength $\approx47$\,m.

So the observed edge wave is neither a density wave nor an edge mode (cold or
pressure).

\section{The static tidal distortion (the resolution)}\label{sec:tidal}

The edge lies $d\approx162$\,km $\approx8.5\,R_H$ from Pan --- the linear tidal
regime ($d\gg R_H$).  Pan's gravity pulls the edge radially, balanced by the
epicyclic restoring force $\kappa^2\delta r$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tidal}
\kappa^2\,\delta r=-\frac{GM_P\,d}{(d^2+y^2)^{3/2}},
\end{equation}
where $y$ is the azimuthal offset from Pan.  This is a \emph{forced epicycle}
with eccentricity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ecc}
e_f=\frac{M_P}{M_S}\Bigl(\frac{a_P}{d}\Bigr)^2\approx5.9\times10^{-6},
\end{equation}
radial amplitude $A=a_P\,e_f=GM_P/(\kappa^2 d^2)$, and azimuthal profile
$\delta r(y)=\delta r_{\max}\,(1+(y/d)^2)^{-3/2}$ (a localized bump of width
$d$).

\section{Observation: extraction from a Cassini image}\label{sec:image}

We extracted the edge-wave parameters directly from NASA's public-domain image
PIA08257 (``Encke Waves'', $1$\,km/px original, stretched $4\times$ in the radial
direction), detecting the gap edges and fitting the strongest sinusoid:
azimuthal wavelength $\lambda_\theta\approx260$--$320$\,km and radial amplitude
$A\approx0.65$\,km, hence $m\approx2800$ (from $m=2\pi r/\lambda_\theta$).

\section{Discussion: what the HTWR framework does and does not contribute}
\label{sec:discussion}

The HTWR framework contributes the \emph{arithmetic skeleton}: the resonance
ladder is a Farey sequence realized as Ford circles on $\Ht^2$, with the
corotation point as the cusp.  This is a \emph{physical realization} of the
rational-approximation structure that the HTWR axioms (state space
$\PP^1(\Q)$, logarithmic-Weil-height calibration) abstract --- not a new theorem,
and not the mechanism of the edge wave itself.

The edge-wave \emph{mechanism} is standard linear celestial mechanics (forced
epicycle), where the HTWR framework has no role.  The two layers answer
different questions: \emph{where} the gap is (resonance overlap, the Farey
pile-up), and \emph{what shape} its edge has (static tidal distortion).

\begin{status}
\emph{Honest boundary.} The secondary signatures --- ``wavelength varies
smoothly'', ``amplitude grows and decays'', ``undamped over $\gtrsim10$ months''
--- are not captured by the static force balance; they require the full dynamics
(collisional damping and multi-$m$ superposition).  These remain open

\end{status}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The Encke gap's scalloped edges are a two-layer phenomenon: an \emph{arithmetic
skeleton} (Pan's Lindblad resonances form the Farey ladder $x=m/(m\pm1)$,
accumulating at corotation as Ford circles --- a concrete instance of the HTWR
rational-approximation structure), and a \emph{dynamical layer} (the observed
edge wave is Pan's static tidal distortion, a forced epicycle of amplitude
$GM_P/(\kappa^2d^2)\approx0.79$\,km, matching the observed $\approx0.65$\,km).
The framework's value here is exemplary: it turns a resonance catalogue into a
geometric object on $\PP^1(\Q)$, while the physics that shapes the edge remains
classical.
