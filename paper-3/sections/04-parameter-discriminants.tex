\subsection{Ambient families and vertical transversality}

An ambient family is the correct starting object; a tube is a conclusion about a
particular incidence inside it.

\begin{definition}[Ambient AEG family]
\label{def:ambient-aeg-family}
An \emph{ambient regular AEG family} over a smooth manifold $B$ consists of a
smooth fiber bundle $q:X\to B$ whose vertical tangent bundle $T^VX$ carries a
smooth orientation, a smooth vertical Riemannian metric $g^V$, and a smooth
function $A:X\to\R$ such that
$(M_b,g_b,A_b;\mu,\lambda)$ is a regular AES for every $b\in B$.  Its assignment
incidence is $\calZ(A)=A^{-1}(0)$.

Equivalently, the structure group of the surface bundle is reduced to
orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms.  Merely choosing an orientation on each
fiber without requiring those choices to vary continuously would not orient
$T^VX$ and is not sufficient here.

More generally, a \emph{field family over an AEG background} is a rank-$r$ real
vector bundle $E\to X$ with a smooth section $s$.  It need not be the real
assignment and need not satisfy the eikonal equation.  A complex-valued field is
the case $r=2$ after forgetting complex scalar multiplication.
\end{definition}

Let $T^VX=\ker(\dd q)$ be the vertical tangent bundle.  At a zero of $s$, choose
any connection to split $TX$ and define the vertical derivative
\[
 D^Vs:T^V_xX\longrightarrow E_x.
\]
Its value at a zero is independent of the chosen local trivialization or
connection: it is the derivative of the restricted section on the fiber.

\begin{theorem}[Parameterized zero-section theorem]
\label{thm:parameterized-zero-section}
Let $q:X\to B$ be a smooth fiber bundle and $E\to X$ a rank-$r$ vector bundle.
Suppose the section $s\in\Gamma(E)$ is vertically transverse to the zero section,
meaning that $D^Vs$ is surjective at every $x\in\calZ(s)$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\calZ(s)$ is a smooth submanifold of $X$ of codimension $r$;
\item the restricted projection
\[
 \pi=q|_{\calZ(s)}:\calZ(s)\longrightarrow B
 \]
is a smooth submersion;
\item if $\pi$ is surjective and proper, then it is a locally trivial smooth
fiber bundle.
\end{enumerate}
The third conclusion has the standard relative form when $X$ has boundary and the
zero incidence is neat, with the boundary restriction also a submersion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Vertical surjectivity implies surjectivity of the full derivative of $s$, so the
zero-section transversality theorem gives the first conclusion.  To prove the
second, take $\xi\in T_bB$ and a lift $w\in T_xX$.  Since $D^Vs$ is surjective,
there is $v\in T_x^VX$ with $D^Vs(v)=-Ds(w)$.  Then
$w+v\in T_x\calZ(s)$ and $Dq(w+v)=\xi$, so $D\pi$ is surjective.  The third
conclusion is Ehresmann's fibration theorem; the neat relative version follows by
the corresponding boundary argument \cite{Ehresmann1952Connexions}.
\end{proof}

The rank is decisive.  If the fibers of $q$ have dimension $m$, then a regular
zero fiber has dimension $m-r$.

\small
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\toprule
Fiber dimension & Field rank & Fiberwise zero & Natural transport \\
\midrule
$2$ & $1$ & curves & component and mapping-class monodromy \\
$2$ & $2$ & points & permutation and braid monodromy \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For an ambient regular AEG family, equation~\eqref{eq:aeg-eikonal} makes
$D^VA=\dd A_b$ nonzero everywhere, so the first two conclusions are automatic at
zero.  Properness is not.

\begin{remark}[The intensity plane is not a base for the zero incidence]
\label{rem:intensity-plane-not-a-base}
Definition~\ref{def:ambient-aeg-family} fixes $(\mu,\lambda)$ outside the base.
Promoting the two intensities to base coordinates does not by itself produce a
larger family of tubes, in either of the two readings available here.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Fixed assignment.}  Let $A$ be a fixed submersion on a fixed surface
and, for each $(\mu,\lambda)$, let the fiber metric be the conformal realization
\eqref{eq:conformal-realization-metric} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:conformal-realization}.  Then $A$ does not depend on
$(\mu,\lambda)$, and the zero incidence is $Z(A)\times\{(\mu,\lambda)\}$.
\item \emph{Fixed metric.}  Let $(M,g)$ be fixed and suppose that each
$(\mu,\lambda)$ carries an assignment satisfying \eqref{eq:aeg-eikonal}.  By the
normal form of Paper~0 \cite{Yuan2026AEGPaperZero}, on a connected complete
boundaryless $M$ that assignment is
\[
 a=\frac{|\mu|}{|\lambda|}\sinh(|\lambda|t)\quad(\lambda\ne0),
 \qquad a=|\mu|\,t\quad(\lambda=0),
\]
where $t$ is the distance to $Z(a)$.  The level sets of $a$, and hence $Z(a)$, are
the same for every $(\mu,\lambda)$; only the arithmetic label attached to each
level changes.
\end{enumerate}
In both readings the tube over the intensity plane is a product, and the parameter
moves the metric or the labels rather than the carrier.  A construction intended to
produce new tubes from a base parameter must therefore vary something other than
$(\mu,\lambda)$.  What the intensity axis does move is the subject of
Subsection~\ref{subsec:intensity-collapse}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A length unit and a dimensionless ratio]
\label{rem:intensity-plane-factorization}
The axioms are invariant under the joint rescaling
\begin{equation}
 (g,a;\mu,\lambda)\longmapsto(s^2g,a;\mu/s,\lambda/s),
 \qquad s>0,
 \label{eq:intensity-scaling}
\end{equation}
because both sides of \eqref{eq:aeg-eikonal} are multiplied by $s^{-2}$; the
normalized arithmetic frame is unchanged, and so are $Z(a)$ and the level
foliation.  The ratio
\begin{equation}
 \rho:=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}
 \label{eq:intensity-ratio}
\end{equation}
is invariant, while $\mu$ alone sets the length unit.  On the basic hyperbolic
model the rescaling is visible in coordinates as
\begin{equation}
 g_{\mu,\lambda}=\mu^{-2}g_{1,\rho},
 \qquad
 K=-\lambda^2=-\mu^2\rho^2 ,
 \label{eq:intensity-factorization}
\end{equation}
so that after $\mu$ fixes the unit the intensities contribute one dimensionless
parameter $\rho$, and $\rho^2$ is the curvature measured in units of $\mu$.  The
rescaling is a symmetry of the axioms and not an equivalence of arithmetic
expression spaces: it multiplies every length by $s$, and lengths are observable.
It must therefore not be used to quotient a moduli problem without saying so.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The discriminant is not a single equation}

For finite-dimensional polynomial root families, the algebraic discriminant
detects repeated roots.  A general AEG family has more ways to lose topological
stability.

\begin{definition}[Structural discriminant]
\label{def:structural-discriminant}
For a parameterized zero problem, the \emph{structural discriminant} is the union
of the following loci in the parameter space, whenever they are present:
\begin{align*}
 \calD_{\mathrm{crit}}
   &=\{b:\text{$D^Vs$ fails to be surjective at some zero over $b$}\},\\
 \calD_{\partial}
   &=\{b:\text{the zero incidence fails the specified neat boundary condition}\},\\
 \calD_{\mathrm{sing}}
   &=\{b:\text{a zero meets the declared singular stratum or the metric degenerates}\},\\
 \calD_{\mathrm{dom}}
   &=\{b:\text{the domain, rank, or regularity class of the field changes}\},\\
 \calD_{\infty}
   &=\{b:\text{$\pi$ is not proper over any neighborhood of $b$}\}.
\end{align*}
If projective continuation is used, its ordinary poles and points at infinity are
recorded separately rather than silently absorbed into $\calD_{\mathrm{sing}}$.
\end{definition}

This definition is a bookkeeping union, not a claim that every stratum is a smooth
hypersurface.  The geometry of its intersections and a canonical Whitney
stratification remain open in general.

For the monic relative divisor of
Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-zero-divisor-decomposition}, the algebraic discriminant
is the scheme-theoretic analogue of the collision/ramification locus, and its
complement supports a finite \'etale root covering.  If $K\subset\C$, then on a
smooth analytic open of $B^{\mathrm{an}}$ where the polynomial is regarded as a
complex rank-$2$ field, this locus is the repeated-root, hence vertical-critical,
part of $\calD_{\mathrm{crit}}$.  Without that analytification and smooth-locus
restriction the two categories are not identified.  The algebraic discriminant
does not see $\calD_\partial$, $\calD_{\mathrm{sing}}$,
$\calD_{\mathrm{dom}}$, or $\calD_\infty$.  Thus arithmetic factorization and
geometric stability fit into the same framework without identifying the
polynomial discriminant with the entire structural discriminant.

\begin{corollary}[Stability off the discriminant]
\label{cor:stability-off-discriminant}
Let $U\subset B$ be connected.  Over $U$, assume that $q:X\to B$ and the
rank-$r$ bundle $E$ remain fixed smooth bundles, that $s$ is vertically transverse
to zero, and that the incidence is surjective and locally proper over $U$.  If a
boundary is present, also assume that the incidence is neat and that its boundary
projection is a submersion.  Then
$\pi:\calZ(s)|_U\to U$ is a smooth fiber bundle.  In particular, the diffeomorphism
type and number of connected components of the fiber are locally constant on $U$.
These are precisely the operative hypotheses encoded by saying that $U$ avoids
the relevant pieces of the structural discriminant.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each $b\in U$, local properness supplies a neighborhood $V$ on which the
restricted incidence projection is proper.  The other hypotheses make that
restriction a surjective submersion, including the relative boundary condition
when needed.  Theorem~\ref{thm:parameterized-zero-section} therefore gives a
bundle chart over a possibly smaller neighborhood of $b$.  Such charts are exactly
the local-triviality assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Escape to infinity without a critical zero}

The following regular AEG family shows why $\calD_\infty$ cannot be omitted.  Set
\[
 h_t(y)=y(ty-1),
 \qquad
 A(x,y,t)=e^x h_t(y),
 \]
and equip each copy of $\R^2$ with the smoothly varying conformal metric
\begin{equation}
 g_t=
 \frac{e^{2x}\bigl(h_t(y)^2+(2ty-1)^2\bigr)}
      {\mu^2+\lambda^2e^{2x}h_t(y)^2}
 (\dd x^2+\dd y^2).
 \label{eq:escape-family-metric}
\end{equation}
At a zero of $h_t$, its derivative $2ty-1$ equals $-1$ or $1$, so it never
vanishes.  More strongly, $h_t$ and $2ty-1$ never vanish simultaneously anywhere.
Thus every slice is a regular AES by Theorem~\ref{thm:conformal-realization}.

Its zero fibers are
\begin{equation}
 Z(A_t)=
 \begin{cases}
  \{y=0\},&t=0,\\
  \{y=0\}\amalg\{y=1/t\},&t\ne0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:escape-zero-fibers}
\end{equation}
The total zero incidence is the disjoint union of the plane $y=0$ and the
hyperbolic sheet $ty=1$.  It is smooth, and its projection to $t$ is a submersion.
Nevertheless the second component escapes to spatial infinity as $t\to0$.

\begin{proposition}[Nonproper topology change]
\label{prop:nonproper-escape}
The family \eqref{eq:escape-family-metric} has no critical zero and no singular
metric, but the number of zero components changes at $t=0$.  Its incidence
projection is not proper over any neighborhood of $0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only the last statement remains.  For $t_j\to0$ with $t_j\ne0$, the points
$(0,1/t_j,t_j)$ lie in the incidence above a compact parameter interval and have
no convergent subsequence.  Hence the inverse image of that interval is not
compact.
\end{proof}

Thus topology change does not require $\dd A_t=0$ at a finite zero.  It may occur at
infinity.  Conversely, a smooth total incidence does not imply a tube.

\subsection{Boundary control}

If a surface fiber has boundary, a zero curve may be closed or may end on the
boundary.  For a stable relative tube one requires the incidence to meet
$\partial^VX$ neatly and the restriction
$\pi:\partial\calZ\to B$ to remain a proper submersion.  Without this condition a
zero component can be created by tangency to the boundary even though the interior
field remains regular.  Such a parameter lies in $\calD_\partial$, not in
$\calD_{\mathrm{crit}}$.

\subsection{A parameter axis with no discriminant}
\label{subsec:intensity-collapse}

The structural discriminant of Definition~\ref{def:structural-discriminant}
records every way a parameterized zero problem can lose stability.  The family
below shows that a parameter axis may avoid that discriminant completely and still
degenerate: no zero moves, no component is created or destroyed, and the metric
itself collapses onto the carrier.  Write
\begin{equation}
 g_\lambda=\frac{|\dd A|_h^2}{\mu^2+\lambda^2A^2}\,h
 \label{eq:intensity-collapse-metric}
\end{equation}
for the conformal realization of Theorem~\ref{thm:conformal-realization} with
background metric $h$, submersion $A$, and parameter $\lambda>0$.

\begin{proposition}[Collapse onto the carrier]
\label{prop:intensity-collapse}
Let $M$ be a compact oriented surface with boundary, $h$ a Riemannian metric on
$M$, and $A\in C^\infty(M,\R)$ with $\dd A\ne0$ everywhere and $0\in A(M)$; put
$\Sigma=A^{-1}(0)$.  For $\lambda>0$ let $g_\lambda$ be given by
\eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-metric}.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(M,g_\lambda,A;\mu,\lambda)$ is a regular AES for every $\lambda>0$;
\item the induced metric on $\Sigma$ is
  $g_\infty:=\mu^{-2}|\dd A|_h^2\,h|_\Sigma$, independent of $\lambda$, and the
  inclusion $\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,g_\lambda)$ is isometric for every $\lambda$;
\item for every $p\in M$,
  \begin{equation}
   \operatorname{dist}_{g_\lambda}(p,\Sigma)
    \le\frac1\lambda\operatorname{arcsinh}
      \frac{\lambda|A(p)|}{\mu},
   \label{eq:intensity-collapse-distance}
  \end{equation}
  so $(M,g_\lambda)$ converges to $(\Sigma,g_\infty)$ in the
  Gromov--Hausdorff sense at rate $O(\lambda^{-1}\log\lambda)$;
\item $\operatorname{area}(M,g_\lambda)
  =\pi L_{g_\infty}(\Sigma)\,\lambda^{-1}
   +O(\lambda^{-2}\log\lambda)$, where $L_{g_\infty}$ is length in
  $g_\infty$;
\item the limit carries the constant assignment $0$ and satisfies
  $|\nabla A|_{g_\infty}=0$ at every point, so it is not a regular AES.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) is Theorem~\ref{thm:conformal-realization} applied to the submersion $A$ and
the background $h$: the prefactor in \eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-metric} is
positive and smooth, so $g_\lambda$ is a Riemannian metric and the eikonal identity
holds.

(2) On $\Sigma$ one has $A=0$, hence
$g_\lambda|_\Sigma=\mu^{-2}|\dd A|_h^2\,h|_\Sigma=g_\infty$ by
\eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-metric}.  The induced metric of $\Sigma$ in
$(M,g_\lambda)$ is therefore $\lambda$-independent, so the inclusion is isometric.

(3) Follow the $h$-normal path from $p$ to $\Sigma$; it exists because $\dd A$ does
not vanish.  Along that path $\dd s_h=\dd A/|\dd A|_h$ in absolute value, so its
$g_\lambda$-length is
\[
 \int_0^{|A(p)|}
 \frac{|\dd A|_h}{\sqrt{\mu^2+\lambda^2A^2}}\,\frac{\dd A}{|\dd A|_h}
 =\frac1\lambda\operatorname{arcsinh}\frac{\lambda|A(p)|}{\mu},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-distance}.  Since $A$ is bounded on the
compact $M$, every point of $M$ lies within
$\varepsilon(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{-1}\log\lambda)$ of the isometrically embedded
$\Sigma$.  A correspondence that sends each point of $M$ to a nearest point of
$\Sigma$ and fixes $\Sigma$ therefore has distortion at most $2\varepsilon$, which
gives the stated Gromov--Hausdorff rate.

(4) The coarea formula with respect to $h$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{area}(M,g_\lambda)
 =\int_{\R}\frac{I(c)}{\mu^2+\lambda^2c^2}\,\dd c,
 \qquad
 I(c)=\int_{A=c}|\dd A|_h\,\dd\mathcal H^1 ,
\]
with $I$ smooth and compactly supported in the image of $A$.  The value $I(0)$ is
$\mu\,L_{g_\infty}(\Sigma)$, because the arclength element of $g_\infty$ is
$\mu^{-1}|\dd A|_h\,\dd\mathcal H^1$.  Splitting off $I(0)$ gives
$\pi I(0)/(\mu\lambda)$ up to an $O(\lambda^{-2})$ correction coming from the
finite range of $A$, while the remainder is bounded by
$O\!\left(\lambda^{-2}\log\lambda\right)$ since $|I(c)-I(0)|=O(|c|)$ and
$\int|c|\,\dd c/(\mu^2+\lambda^2c^2)=O(\lambda^{-2}\log\lambda)$ over a bounded
interval.  This is the stated asymptotic.

(5) $A$ is constant on $\Sigma$, so its intrinsic gradient there vanishes at every
point, whereas the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:aeg-eikonal} is at least
$\mu^2>0$.  The limit violates the eikonal identity and is not a regular AES.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Both hypotheses are active]
\label{rem:intensity-collapse-hypotheses}
The condition $\dd A\ne0$ cannot be dropped: at a critical point of $A$ the
prefactor in \eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-metric} vanishes and $g_\lambda$ is not a
metric.  On a compact surface without boundary a smooth $A$ always has a critical
point, so the compact case must carry a boundary; this is the obstruction that also
excludes a closed regular AES in Paper~0.  For an ambient family the statement is
applied fiberwise, with $\dd A$ replaced by the vertical derivative $D^VA$ and $h$
by the vertical metric, and with the conclusion restricted to the regular part
$M\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$, where
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}=\{D^VA=0\}$ is exactly the stratum that
Proposition~\ref{prop:polynomial-carrier-thread} declares singular.  There the
limit is $\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{warning}[A second collapse that is not this one]
\label{warn:intensity-collapse-two-readings}
The family \eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-metric} keeps $A$ and the conformal
background $h$ fixed and lets the metric move.  A different family keeps the
assignment of the basic model fixed in its $(x,y)$ chart and lets the metric move.
The two are distinguished by which datum is held fixed, not by whether the
conformal class moves.  For that second family $g_{\mu,\lambda}=\mu^{-2}g_{1,\rho}$ degenerates as
$\rho\to\infty$ to the rank-one tensor $\mu^{-2}\dd x^2/y^2$: the surviving
direction is the additive orbit $\partial_x$, the collapsing direction is the
multiplicative orbit $\partial_y$, and the zero geodesic $\{x=0\}$ is transverse to
the surviving direction, so it is squeezed out.  No Gromov--Hausdorff limit is
asserted for this second family, because the surviving length scale $1/y$ is
unbounded.

The distinction is not a technicality, and it survives a change of chart.  In the
horocyclic coordinates of Paper~0 the pullback of the basic model is
$\widehat g_\lambda=e^{-2\lambda v}\dd u^2/\mu^2+\dd v^2$, which appears to collapse
the $\partial_u$ direction instead.  The change of coordinates depends on
$\lambda$, so the two computations concern different families of metrics at
different points.  A degeneration must always be stated together with the
identification that is held fixed.
\end{warning}

Consequently the intensity axis meets no interior locus of the structural
discriminant: the zero incidence is a product with base $\R_{>0}$, every slice
carries the same carrier, and the total space of the incidence is
$\Sigma\times\R_{>0}$.  The only locus it can meet is the boundary one,
$\calD_\partial$, and only when $\Sigma$ fails to be transverse to $\partial M$.
What the five loci cannot see is precisely the metric-only degeneration of
Proposition~\ref{prop:intensity-collapse}.

\end{problem}
