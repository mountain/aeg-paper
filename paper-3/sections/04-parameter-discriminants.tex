\subsection{Ambient families and vertical transversality}

An ambient family is the correct starting object; a tube is a conclusion about a
particular incidence inside it.

\begin{definition}[Ambient AEG family]
\label{def:ambient-aeg-family}
An \emph{ambient regular AEG family} over a smooth manifold $B$ consists of a
smooth fiber bundle $q:X\to B$ whose vertical tangent bundle $T^VX$ carries a
smooth orientation, a smooth vertical Riemannian metric $g^V$, and a smooth
function $A:X\to\R$ such that
$(M_b,g_b,A_b;\mu,\lambda)$ is a regular AES for every $b\in B$.  Its assignment
incidence is $\calZ(A)=A^{-1}(0)$.

Equivalently, the structure group of the surface bundle is reduced to
orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms.  Merely choosing an orientation on each
fiber without requiring those choices to vary continuously would not orient
$T^VX$ and is not sufficient here.

More generally, a \emph{field family over an AEG background} is a rank-$r$ real
vector bundle $E\to X$ with a smooth section $s$.  It need not be the real
assignment and need not satisfy the eikonal equation.  A complex-valued field is
the case $r=2$ after forgetting complex scalar multiplication.
\end{definition}

Let $T^VX=\ker(\dd q)$ be the vertical tangent bundle.  At a zero of $s$, choose
any connection to split $TX$ and define the vertical derivative
\[
 D^Vs:T^V_xX\longrightarrow E_x.
\]
Its value at a zero is independent of the chosen local trivialization or
connection: it is the derivative of the restricted section on the fiber.

\begin{theorem}[Parameterized zero-section theorem]
\label{thm:parameterized-zero-section}
Let $q:X\to B$ be a smooth fiber bundle and $E\to X$ a rank-$r$ vector bundle.
Suppose the section $s\in\Gamma(E)$ is vertically transverse to the zero section,
meaning that $D^Vs$ is surjective at every $x\in\calZ(s)$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\calZ(s)$ is a smooth submanifold of $X$ of codimension $r$;
\item the restricted projection
\[
 \pi=q|_{\calZ(s)}:\calZ(s)\longrightarrow B
 \]
is a smooth submersion;
\item if $\pi$ is surjective and proper, then it is a locally trivial smooth
fiber bundle.
\end{enumerate}
The third conclusion has the standard relative form when $X$ has boundary and the
zero incidence is neat, with the boundary restriction also a submersion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Vertical surjectivity implies surjectivity of the full derivative of $s$, so the
zero-section transversality theorem gives the first conclusion.  To prove the
second, take $\xi\in T_bB$ and a lift $w\in T_xX$.  Since $D^Vs$ is surjective,
there is $v\in T_x^VX$ with $D^Vs(v)=-Ds(w)$.  Then
$w+v\in T_x\calZ(s)$ and $Dq(w+v)=\xi$, so $D\pi$ is surjective.  The third
conclusion is Ehresmann's fibration theorem; the neat relative version follows by
the corresponding boundary argument \cite{Ehresmann1952Connexions}.
\end{proof}

The rank is decisive.  If the fibers of $q$ have dimension $m$, then a regular
zero fiber has dimension $m-r$.

For an ambient regular AEG family, equation~\eqref{eq:aeg-eikonal} makes
$D^VA=\dd A_b$ nonzero everywhere, so the first two conclusions are automatic at
zero.  Properness is not.

\begin{remark}[Fixed intensities versus a fixed zero carrier]
\label{rem:intensity-plane-not-a-base}
Definition~\ref{def:ambient-aeg-family} fixes $(\mu,\lambda)$ outside the base.
If these intensities are instead parameters, a fixed carrier requires an
additional choice of data; it does not follow from the eikonal identity alone.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Fixed assignment.}  Fix a submersion $A$ and a background metric $h$.
The conformal realization \eqref{eq:conformal-realization-metric} changes the
metric with $(\mu,\lambda)$, while its zero incidence is exactly
$Z(A)\times B$ over the chosen intensity parameter domain $B$.
\item \emph{Fixed metric and fixed rectified assignment.}  Fix $(M,g)$ and one
smooth function $t$ with $|\nabla t|_g=1$.  Then
\[
 a_{\mu,\lambda}=\frac{|\mu|}{|\lambda|}\sinh(|\lambda|t)
 \quad(\lambda\ne0),\qquad a_{\mu,0}=|\mu|t
\]
satisfies the eikonal identity, and all these assignments have the same level
foliation and zero set $\{t=0\}$.  In the complete normal form of Paper~0
\cite{Yuan2026AEGPaperZero}, $t$ is a \emph{signed} normal coordinate determined
by the assignment.  Fixing the metric alone does not fix that coordinate.
\end{enumerate}
For example, on the complete Euclidean plane, for any smooth real function
$b(\mu,\lambda)$ and $\mu,\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}
 a_{\mu,\lambda}(x,y)
 =\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\sinh\bigl(\lambda(x-b(\mu,\lambda))\bigr)
 \label{eq:intensity-moving-zero}
\end{equation}
has $|\nabla a_{\mu,\lambda}|^2=\mu^2+\lambda^2a_{\mu,\lambda}^2$, but its zero
line is $x=b(\mu,\lambda)$.  The total incidence in this example is abstractly
a product, but it is not the same embedded carrier in every fiber.  Thus the two
fixed-data constructions above give products; they do not constitute a no-go
theorem for all intensity-indexed assignments on a fixed metric.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A length unit and a dimensionless ratio]
\label{rem:intensity-plane-factorization}
The axioms are invariant under the joint rescaling
\begin{equation}
 (g,a;\mu,\lambda)\longmapsto(s^2g,a;\mu/s,\lambda/s),
 \qquad s>0,
 \label{eq:intensity-scaling}
\end{equation}
because both sides of \eqref{eq:aeg-eikonal} are multiplied by $s^{-2}$; the
arithmetic frame is multiplied by $s^{-1}$, so its directions are unchanged,
as are $Z(a)$ and the level foliation.  The ratio
\begin{equation}
 \rho:=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}
 \label{eq:intensity-ratio}
\end{equation}
is invariant, while $\mu$ alone sets the length unit.  On the basic hyperbolic
model the rescaling is visible in coordinates as
\begin{equation}
 g_{\mu,\lambda}=\mu^{-2}g_{1,\rho},
 \qquad
 K=-\lambda^2=-\mu^2\rho^2 ,
 \label{eq:intensity-factorization}
\end{equation}
so that after $\mu$ fixes the unit the intensities contribute one dimensionless
parameter $\rho$, and $\rho^2$ is the curvature measured in units of $\mu$.  The
rescaling is a symmetry of the axioms and not an equivalence of arithmetic
expression spaces: it multiplies every length by $s$, and lengths are observable.
It must therefore not be used to quotient a moduli problem without saying so.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The discriminant is not a single equation}

For finite-dimensional polynomial root families, the algebraic discriminant
detects repeated roots.  A general AEG family has more ways to lose topological
stability.

\begin{definition}[Structural discriminant]
\label{def:structural-discriminant}
For a parameterized zero problem, the \emph{structural discriminant} is the union
of the following loci in the parameter space, whenever they are present:
\begin{align*}
 \calD_{\mathrm{crit}}
   &=\{b:\text{$D^Vs$ fails to be surjective at some zero over $b$}\},\\
 \calD_{\partial}
   &=\{b:\text{the zero incidence fails the specified neat boundary condition}\},\\
 \calD_{\mathrm{sing}}
   &=\{b:\text{a zero meets the declared singular stratum or the metric degenerates}\},\\
 \calD_{\mathrm{dom}}
   &=\{b:\text{the domain, rank, or regularity class of the field changes}\},\\
 \calD_{\infty}
   &=\{b:\text{$\pi$ is not proper over any neighborhood of $b$}\}.
\end{align*}
If projective continuation is used, its ordinary poles and points at infinity are
recorded separately rather than silently absorbed into $\calD_{\mathrm{sing}}$.
\end{definition}

This definition is a bookkeeping union, not a claim that every stratum is a smooth
hypersurface.  The geometry of its intersections and a canonical Whitney
stratification remain open in general.

For the monic relative divisor of
Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-zero-divisor-decomposition}, the algebraic discriminant
is the scheme-theoretic analogue of the collision/ramification locus, and its
complement supports a finite \'etale root covering.  If $K\subset\C$, then on a
smooth analytic open of $B^{\mathrm{an}}$ where the polynomial is regarded as a
complex rank-$2$ field, this locus is the repeated-root, hence vertical-critical,
part of $\calD_{\mathrm{crit}}$.  Without that analytification and smooth-locus
restriction the two categories are not identified.  The algebraic discriminant
does not see $\calD_\partial$, $\calD_{\mathrm{sing}}$,
$\calD_{\mathrm{dom}}$, or $\calD_\infty$.  Thus arithmetic factorization and
geometric stability fit into the same framework without identifying the
polynomial discriminant with the entire structural discriminant.

\begin{corollary}[Stability off the discriminant]
\label{cor:stability-off-discriminant}
Let $U\subset B$ be connected.  Over $U$, assume that $q:X\to B$ and the
rank-$r$ bundle $E$ remain fixed smooth bundles, that $s$ is vertically transverse
to zero, and that the incidence is surjective and locally proper over $U$.  If a
boundary is present, also assume that the incidence is neat and that its boundary
projection is a submersion.  Then
$\pi:\calZ(s)|_U\to U$ is a smooth fiber bundle.  In particular, the diffeomorphism
type and number of connected components of the fiber are locally constant on $U$.
These are precisely the operative hypotheses encoded by saying that $U$ avoids
the relevant pieces of the structural discriminant.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each $b\in U$, local properness supplies a neighborhood $V$ on which the
restricted incidence projection is proper.  The other hypotheses make that
restriction a surjective submersion, including the relative boundary condition
when needed.  Theorem~\ref{thm:parameterized-zero-section} therefore gives a
bundle chart over a possibly smaller neighborhood of $b$.  Such charts are exactly
the local-triviality assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Escape to infinity without a critical zero}

The following regular AEG family shows why $\calD_\infty$ cannot be omitted.  Set
\[
 h_t(y)=y(ty-1),
 \qquad
 A(x,y,t)=e^x h_t(y),
 \]
and equip each copy of $\R^2$ with the smoothly varying conformal metric
\begin{equation}
 g_t=
 \frac{e^{2x}\bigl(h_t(y)^2+(2ty-1)^2\bigr)}
      {\mu^2+\lambda^2e^{2x}h_t(y)^2}
 (\dd x^2+\dd y^2).
 \label{eq:escape-family-metric}
\end{equation}
At a zero of $h_t$, its derivative $2ty-1$ equals $-1$ or $1$, so it never
vanishes.  More strongly, $h_t$ and $2ty-1$ never vanish simultaneously anywhere.
Thus every slice is a regular AES by Theorem~\ref{thm:conformal-realization}.

Its zero fibers are
\begin{equation}
 Z(A_t)=
 \begin{cases}
  \{y=0\},&t=0,\\
  \{y=0\}\amalg\{y=1/t\},&t\ne0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:escape-zero-fibers}
\end{equation}
The total zero incidence is the disjoint union of the plane $y=0$ and the
hyperbolic sheet $ty=1$.  It is smooth, and its projection to $t$ is a submersion.
Nevertheless the second component escapes to spatial infinity as $t\to0$.

\begin{proposition}[Nonproper topology change]
\label{prop:nonproper-escape}
The family \eqref{eq:escape-family-metric} has no critical zero and no singular
metric, but the number of zero components changes at $t=0$.  Its incidence
projection is not proper over any neighborhood of $0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only the last statement remains.  For $t_j\to0$ with $t_j\ne0$, the points
$(0,1/t_j,t_j)$ lie in the incidence above a compact parameter interval and have
no convergent subsequence.  Hence the inverse image of that interval is not
compact.
\end{proof}

Thus topology change does not require $\dd A_t=0$ at a finite zero.  It may occur at
infinity.  Conversely, a smooth total incidence does not imply a tube.

\subsection{Boundary control}

If a surface fiber has boundary, a zero curve may be closed or may end on the
boundary.  For a stable relative tube one requires the incidence to meet
$\partial^VX$ neatly and the restriction
$\pi:\partial\calZ\to B$ to remain a proper submersion.  Without this condition a
zero component can be created by tangency to the boundary even though the interior
field remains regular.  Such a parameter lies in $\calD_\partial$, not in
$\calD_{\mathrm{crit}}$.

\subsection{A parameter axis with fixed zero incidence}
\label{subsec:intensity-collapse}

A fixed zero incidence does not imply a fixed metric-space carrier in the limit.
For a fixed background metric $h$, submersion $A$, and $\mu>0$, put
\begin{equation}
 g_\lambda=\frac{|\dd A|_h^2}{\mu^2+\lambda^2A^2}\,h,
 \qquad \lambda>0.
 \label{eq:intensity-collapse-metric}
\end{equation}
The conformal realization preserves lengths of curves contained in $A=0$, but
ambient paths can leave that set and become shorter.  The distinction controls
the following corrected statement.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed zero tensor, ambient collapse, and area concentration]
\label{prop:intensity-collapse}
Let $M$ be a compact connected oriented smooth surface with boundary, $h$ a
smooth Riemannian metric, and $A\in C^\infty(M,\R)$ with $\dd A\ne0$
everywhere and $0\in A(M)$.  Write $\Sigma=A^{-1}(0)$ and let $d_\lambda$ be
the intrinsic path distance of $g_\lambda$ on $M$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(M,g_\lambda,A;\mu,\lambda)$ is a regular AES.  On every smooth
zero-curve portion the pullback tensor is
\[
 g_\Sigma=\mu^{-2}|\dd A|_h^2h|_{T\Sigma},
\]
independent of $\lambda$.  This is a statement about curve lengths, not an
isometry between the intrinsic distance on $\Sigma$ and $d_\lambda|_{\Sigma}$.
\item For every $p\in M$,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{dist}_{d_\lambda}(p,\Sigma)
 \ \ge\ \frac1\lambda\operatorname{arcsinh}
                  \frac{\lambda|A(p)|}{\mu}.
 \label{eq:intensity-collapse-distance}
\end{equation}
Equality holds if the trajectory of $X=\nabla_h A/|\dd A|_h^2$ from $p$ to
level zero remains in $M$.  If every point has this property, then
\[
 d_{\mathrm{GH}}\bigl((M,d_\lambda),(\Sigma,d_\lambda|_\Sigma)\bigr)
 \le \lambda^{-1}\operatorname{arcsinh}
                    (\lambda\|A\|_\infty/\mu).
\]
Here the metric on $\Sigma$ is the \emph{ambient restricted distance}, which
also varies with $\lambda$.
\item $\operatorname{diam}(M,d_\lambda)\to0$, so $(M,d_\lambda)$ converges
in the Gromov--Hausdorff sense to a single point.  No uniform rate is asserted
under these hypotheses alone.
\item Define the coarea density, extended by zero outside $A(M)$, by
\[
 I(c)=\int_{A=c}|\dd A|_h\,\dd\mathcal H_h^1
 \quad\hbox{for almost every }c.
\]
If, for some $\delta>0$ and constants $I_+,I_-$,
$I(c)=I_++O(c)$ for $0<c<\delta$ and
$I(c)=I_-+O(|c|)$ for $-\delta<c<0$, then
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{area}(M,g_\lambda)
 =\frac{\pi(I_++I_-)}{2\mu\lambda}
       +O(\lambda^{-2}\log\lambda).
 \label{eq:intensity-area-sided}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\Sigma$ is a smooth zero curve and
$I_+=I_-=\mu L_{g_\Sigma}(\Sigma)$, the leading term is
$\pi L_{g_\Sigma}(\Sigma)/\lambda$.  If only one side is present with that
density, its coefficient is half as large.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) The prefactor in \eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-metric} is positive and smooth;
Theorem~\ref{thm:conformal-realization} gives the eikonal identity.  Restriction
to $A=0$ gives the displayed pullback tensor.  It fixes intrinsic curve lengths
but places no lower bound on the lengths of competing paths in $M$.

(2) Set $F_\lambda(u)=\lambda^{-1}\operatorname{arcsinh}(\lambda u/\mu)$.
Directly from the eikonal identity,
$|\dd(F_\lambda\circ A)|_{g_\lambda}=1$.  Integrating along any path from $p$
to $\Sigma$ gives \eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-distance}.  Along an admissible
trajectory of $X$ one has $A=c$ as parameter and
\[
 \dd s_{g_\lambda}=\frac{|\dd c|}{\sqrt{\mu^2+\lambda^2c^2}}.
\]
Its length attains the lower bound.  If all these trajectories stay in $M$,
$\Sigma$ is within the stated Hausdorff distance in the common metric space
$(M,d_\lambda)$.  This bounds the Gromov--Hausdorff distance to the metric
\emph{subset} $(\Sigma,d_\lambda|_\Sigma)$, not to its intrinsic curve metric.

(3) Put $g_0=\mu^{-2}|\dd A|_h^2h$ and write $d_0$ for its path distance.
Then $g_\lambda\le g_0$.  For any $\varepsilon>0$, choose a finite
$\varepsilon$-net for $(M,d_0)$ with centers in
$\operatorname{int}(M)\setminus\Sigma$, a dense subset.  The interior of a
connected smooth manifold with boundary is connected.  Join the centers to a
fixed center by finitely many smooth interior paths transverse to the
smooth interior zero set.  Each such path meets $\Sigma$ at only finitely many
points.  Along it, the $g_\lambda$ speed tends to zero almost everywhere and
is bounded by its integrable $g_0$ speed.  Dominated convergence makes each of
these finitely many path lengths tend to zero.  Every point of $M$ is within
$\varepsilon$ in $d_\lambda$ of a center, whence
$\operatorname{diam}(M,d_\lambda)\le2\varepsilon+o(1)$.
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the claim.  The GH distance to a
single point is half the diameter.

(4) Coarea gives exactly
\[
 \operatorname{area}(M,g_\lambda)
 =\int_{\R}\frac{I(c)}{\mu^2+\lambda^2c^2}\,\dd c.
\]
The density is integrable, since
$\int I(c)\,\dd c=\int_M|\dd A|_h^2\,\dd\operatorname{vol}_h<\infty$.
The part with $|c|\ge\delta$ is $O(\lambda^{-2})$.  On either side of zero,
\[
 \int_0^\delta\frac{\dd c}{\mu^2+\lambda^2c^2}
   =\frac{\pi}{2\mu\lambda}+O(\lambda^{-2}),
 \qquad
 \int_0^\delta\frac{c\,\dd c}{\mu^2+\lambda^2c^2}
   =O(\lambda^{-2}\log\lambda).
\]
Insert the two one-sided estimates.  On a smooth zero curve,
$I(0)=\mu L_{g_\Sigma}(\Sigma)$; equality with the side limits is an
additional assertion, not a consequence of $\dd A\ne0$ at a boundary point.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A cylinder whose zero circle also collapses in ambient distance]
\label{ex:intensity-cylinder-shortcut}
Let $M=[-1,1]\times(\R/2\pi\Z)$, $h=\dd t^2+\dd\theta^2$, and $A=t$.
Then $g_\lambda=(\dd t^2+\dd\theta^2)/(\mu^2+\lambda^2t^2)$ and the
intrinsic length of the zero circle is $2\pi/\mu$.  Nevertheless any two points
can be joined by going radially to $t=1$, along that circle, and radially back;
therefore
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(M,d_\lambda)
 \le\frac{4}{\lambda}\operatorname{arcsinh}(\lambda/\mu)
       +\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\mu^2+\lambda^2}}.
\]
For antipodal points on the zero circle the same path has length at most
$2\lambda^{-1}\operatorname{arcsinh}(\lambda/\mu)
+\pi/\sqrt{\mu^2+\lambda^2}\to0$, whereas their intrinsic zero-circle
distance is $\pi/\mu$.  Thus even global gradient access does not yield an
ambient isometric copy of the zero circle or a GH limit equal to that circle.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Boundary access and one-sided area]
\label{ex:intensity-boundary-controls}
Two separate boundary effects must be retained.
First take the closed Euclidean unit disk, $A(x,y)=x+1/2$, and
$p=(0,19/20)$.  The horizontal gradient segment toward $x=-1/2$ leaves the disk,
since $(-1/2)^2+(19/20)^2>1$.  The lower bound
\eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-distance} is strict: a shortest path exists in
this compact smooth Riemannian domain, and equality in the integrated
unit-gradient inequality would force it to be that horizontal monotone segment.
The zero curve meets the boundary transversely, so boundary transversality alone
does not supply gradient access.

Second take $M=[0,1]\times(\R/2\pi\Z)$, $h=\dd t^2+\dd\theta^2$,
$A=t$, and $\mu=1$.  The zero circle has intrinsic length $2\pi$, but
\[
 \operatorname{area}(M,g_\lambda)
 =\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\arctan\lambda
 =\frac{\pi^2}{\lambda}+O(\lambda^{-2}),
\]
not $2\pi^2/\lambda$.  Here $I_+=2\pi$ and $I_-=0$; the missing side of
the zero level changes the leading coefficient.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Boundary, compactness, and the assignment in the limit]
\label{rem:intensity-collapse-hypotheses}
The condition $\dd A\ne0$ ensures a metric, but does not itself ensure that
$\Sigma$ is a smooth one-manifold at the boundary.  For example, $A=x+1$ on
the closed unit disk has a single boundary zero.  Two-sided smooth coarea data
near zero are available if zero is an interior value and
$A|_{\partial M}$ is transverse to zero (including an interior zero curve
disjoint from the boundary).  Indeed a vector field $V$ with $\dd A(V)=1$
can then be chosen tangent to $\partial M$ near zero; its flow identifies the
nearby fibers smoothly, so $I$ is smooth there.  A zero boundary component with
a one-sided collar instead contributes only its available side.  These are separate from gradient
access.  Bounded range of a submersion is not a substitute for compactness.

For an ambient family, the conformal realization and the pullback-tensor identity
apply on the regular part with $\dd A$ replaced by $D^VA$.  Removing the critical
set generally destroys compactness, so it does not automatically preserve the
collapse or area conclusions.  A single-point metric limit is not a regular
two-dimensional AES.  Moreover the fixed nonconstant assignments $A$ do not
descend uniformly to an assignment on that point: their oscillation is fixed
and positive, so $\inf_{c\in\R}\|A-c\|_\infty=\operatorname{osc}(A)/2>0$.  The identity $A|_\Sigma=0$ is a restriction to the zero
set, not convergence of the functions on all of $M$.
\end{remark}

\begin{warning}[A second collapse that is not this one]
\label{warn:intensity-collapse-two-readings}
The family \eqref{eq:intensity-collapse-metric} keeps $A$ and the conformal
background $h$ fixed and lets the metric move.  A different family keeps the
assignment of the basic model fixed in its $(x,y)$ chart and lets the metric move.
The two are distinguished by which datum is held fixed, not by whether the
conformal class moves.  For that second family $g_{\mu,\lambda}=\mu^{-2}g_{1,\rho}$ degenerates as
$\rho\to\infty$ to the rank-one tensor $\mu^{-2}\dd x^2/y^2$: the surviving
direction is the additive orbit $\partial_x$, the collapsing direction is the
multiplicative orbit $\partial_y$, and the zero geodesic $\{x=0\}$ is transverse to
the surviving direction, so it is squeezed out.  No Gromov--Hausdorff limit is
asserted for this second family, because the surviving length scale $1/y$ is
unbounded.

The distinction is not a technicality, and it survives a change of chart.  In the
horocyclic coordinates of Paper~0 the pullback of the basic model is
$\widehat g_\lambda=e^{-2\lambda v}\dd u^2/\mu^2+\dd v^2$, which appears to collapse
the $\partial_u$ direction instead.  The change of coordinates depends on
$\lambda$, so the two computations concern different families of metrics at
different points.  A degeneration must always be stated together with the
identification that is held fixed.
\end{warning}

For the fixed-assignment construction the zero incidence is
$\Sigma\times\R_{>0}$.  When $\Sigma$ is neat and the boundary restriction is
regular, this axis has no event in the structural discriminant.  The
single-point degeneration of Proposition~\ref{prop:intensity-collapse} is
metric information not detected by that fixed zero incidence.  If boundary
regularity fails, that defect must still be recorded; the product description
does not remove it.
