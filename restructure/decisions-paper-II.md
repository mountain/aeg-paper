# Paper II Decision Record

**Paper:** *Arithmetic Expression Geometry II: Hyperbolic Real Function Theory*

**Decision date:** 2026-08-06

**Baseline:** `61ff60d`

**Status:** integrated manuscript decisions

This record fixes the conventions used by the canonical Paper II manuscript.  It is
task-specific and remains subordinate to the restructuring authorities.

## D2-01 — Two analytic carriers

Paper II treats the regular AES surface and the contact state space as distinct
analytic objects.

- Surface fields are written `X_u,X_v`; contact lifts are written `D_u,D_v`.
- The surface metric and orientation already determine its complex structure.
- The contact branch explicitly chooses a horizontal metric, compatible rotation,
  measure, and operator domain.
- No local horizontal surface is asserted in the contact case when
  `mu lambda != 0`; nonintegrability rules it out.

This decision resolves OQ-031 for the two-dimensional surface and makes the extra
choice in OQ-032 explicit for the contact branch.

## D2-02 — Laplacian sign

The geometric Laplacians use the divergence-of-gradient sign.  Consequently
`Delta_g` and `Delta_C` are nonpositive on compactly supported functions, while
`-Delta_g` and `-Delta_C` are the nonnegative energy operators.

The pointwise Paper I identity remains `Delta_g a = 2 lambda^2 a`.  It is not an
`L^2` eigenvalue statement.

## D2-03 — Drift belongs to the variational operator

If `[X_u,X_v]=c_u X_u+c_v X_v`, the surface operator is

```text
Delta_g = X_u^2 + X_v^2 - c_v X_u + c_u X_v.
```

For contact volume, the contact operator is

```text
Delta_C = D_u^2 + D_v^2 + lambda D_v.
```

The expressions without these drift terms are called raw sums of squares.  They are
used only where their formal products are intended.

## D2-04 — Measures and domains are part of the statement

- Surface adjoints are taken with respect to Riemannian area.
- Contact adjoints are taken with respect to the positive contact volume
  `|alpha wedge d alpha|`.
- Differential operators begin on compactly supported smooth functions.
- The nonnegative contact energy is closed by the Friedrichs construction.

No essential-self-adjointness or spectral-completeness claim is inferred from the
differential formulas.

## D2-05 — Surface arithmetic holomorphicity

The surface operator
`bar partial_AEG = (X_u+iX_v)/2` differs from the ordinary oriented-surface
`bar partial` by a nowhere-zero unitary gauge factor.  Their kernels agree.

The manuscript therefore claims an assignment-adapted normalization and explicit
AEG interpretation, not discovery of a new class of holomorphic functions.

## D2-06 — Contact CR factorization is twisted

The contact equation `bar L F=0` implies both a raw curvature-twisted equation and an
adjoint/Reeb-twisted variational equation.  It is not identified with
Laplace--Beltrami harmonicity on a surface.

## D2-07 — Hyperbolic normalization

Throughout the global model, `mu,lambda>0` and

```text
X = (lambda/mu) x,
w = X + i y.
```

Then the metric is `lambda^{-2}` times the standard upper-half-plane metric and
`w = y(i-(lambda/mu)a)`.  Statements in the original `x` coordinate display every
scale factor explicitly.

## D2-08 — Boundary class

The decisive Dirichlet theorem is stated for `C_0(R)` data and solutions continuous
on the closed upper half-plane that vanish at its point at infinity.  This yields a
clean maximum-principle uniqueness class.

Bounded harmonic fields with discontinuous boundary data are treated separately.
The line `y=0` is always called the ideal or conformal boundary, not a finite
Riemannian boundary.

## D2-09 — Dirichlet-to-Neumann normalization

In the normalized `X` coordinate, the boundary operator is
`-partial_y = |D_X|`.  In the original `x` coordinate, the variational conormal is

```text
-(lambda/mu) partial_y = |D_x|.
```

This is the normalization paired with the hyperbolic Dirichlet energy.  The raw
normal derivative in `x` coordinates is not silently renamed as `|D_x|`.

## D2-10 — Explicit solution families

Paper II includes three kinds of genuinely assignment-dependent solutions:

1. holomorphic and positive-frequency functions of
   `w=y(i-(lambda/mu)a)`;
2. the complete family of surface harmonic fields depending only on `a`;
3. logarithmic first integrals and derived CR fields on the contact state space.

Each family states its domain and boundary class.  No mode-completeness theorem is
claimed.

## D2-11 — Affine–Appell terminology

The legacy constant-coefficient span is not invariant under `D_u`.  The canonical
object is a finite filtered `C^infty(u,v)`-module generated by
`B_n=(e^{-lambda v}a)^n`.  It is called an operator-stable family or module, never a
Hilbert, Schauder, or complete basis.

## D2-12 — Claim boundary and provenance

The classical upper-half-plane Poisson and Fourier theorems are proved or transported
with explicit AEG normalizations and are cited as classical ingredients.  Paper II's
contribution is the audited operator distinction, exact transport, domain statement,
assignment-coordinate consequences, and separation of surface and contact branches.

General Green kernels, spectral completeness, singular continuation, tube topology,
projective condensation, and complexity remain outside the manuscript.

## D2-13 — Exported notation takes precedence over provisional names

Where a provisional restructuring outline and the completed Paper I interface use
different symbols, Paper II follows the proved Paper I export.  In particular, the
contact distribution is `\mathcal D=\ker\alpha`, and the complete upper-half-plane
example is called the basic hyperbolic AES rather than being assigned the provisional
index `\mathfrak E_0`.  This avoids silently renaming the upstream canonical source.
The two Paper II branches use `X_u,X_v` and `D_u,D_v` respectively, so the retained
notation does not create an operator ambiguity.
