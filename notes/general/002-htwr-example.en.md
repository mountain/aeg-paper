## I. Model calibration and test target

Take the Pell rational-convergent subsequence $\{x_n\}_{n \ge 1}$ of $\sqrt{2}$ as the standard testbed. Define integer pairs $(P_n, Q_n)$ by $(1 + \sqrt{2})^n = P_n + Q_n\sqrt{2}$, and write $x_n := \frac{P_n}{Q_n} \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})$ (its shifted state $u_n := x_n + 1 = \frac{Q_{n+1}}{Q_n}$ obeys the pure continued-fraction fixed-point recursion $u_{n+1} = 2 + \frac{1}{u_n}$).

1. **Hyperbolic tube calibration**: let $\lambda := 1 + \sqrt{2}$ be the silver ratio, with single-step hyperbolic depth increment $h_0 := \ln \lambda \approx 0.8814$. Since $\gcd(P_n, Q_n) = 1$ and $P_n = \frac{1}{2}[\lambda^n + (1-\sqrt{2})^n] > Q_n > 0$, the information height (hyperbolic radial depth) of the $n$-th state is exactly:

$$I(x_n) = \ln P_n = n h_0 - \ln 2 + o(1)$$

For the target state $x_N$ ($N \gg 1$), denote the endpoint intrinsic depth by $I_* := I(x_N) = N h_0 - \ln 2 + o(1)$.
2. **Local action calibration**: take the normalised gate slope $\alpha_g = \alpha > 0$ and the noise quantum $\epsilon_0 > 0$ (with $\epsilon_0 \ll \alpha h_0$) of Axiom II:
* unary Möbius geodesic gates $\gamma \in \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ (generated by $z \mapsto z \pm 1,\ z \mapsto -1/z$) and the fan-out singularity $\Delta$: $c(z) = \epsilon_0 + \alpha I(z)$;
* binary scattering gates $\star \in \{+, -, \times, \div\}$: $c(z_1, z_2) = \epsilon_0 + \alpha\big(I(z_1) + I(z_2)\big)$.

The sequence admits two orthogonal generating geometries:

* **Zero-genus hyperbolic geodesic flow ($g=0$)**: via the shift $u_n = x_n + 1$, advance serially along $T(u) = 2 + 1/u$ for $N-1$ steps, maintaining a single wire throughout ($m=1$); the total action is quadratic:

$$\mathcal{A}_{\text{CF}}(x_N) = \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} 3\alpha I(u_n) + O(N\epsilon_0) = \frac{3\alpha}{2 h_0} I_*^2 + O(\alpha I_*)$$

* **High-genus Newton tangent folding ($g \ge 1$)**: exactly satisfies the single-wire doubling identity $x_{2n} = \frac{x_n^2 + 2}{2 x_n}$, needing only $\Theta(\log N)$ handle layers to reach the endpoint.

## II. Distributivity breaking and the resolution of the "$\frac{3}{2}I_*$ false barrier"

In examining the Newton doubling step $x_n \mapsto x_{2n}$ (with input depth $L := I(x_n) \approx \frac{1}{2} I_*$), the **distributivity / partial-fraction symmetry breaking** in the suffix context space $\mathcal{K}_1$ directly determines the spatial peak and the wormhole loss:

1. **Undisassembled rational-fraction context $k_{\text{naive}}(\square) = \frac{\square^2 + 2}{2\square}$**:
* requires two $\Delta$ fan-outs producing a three-wire cross-section $(\square, \square, \square)$; the left two paths multiply and plunge into the square abyss $\frac{P_n^2}{Q_n^2}$ of depth $2L$, while the right path suspends the denominator $2x_n$ of depth $L$.
* before the division gate closes, the instantaneous total flux of the two-tube cross-section $(P_n^2 + 2Q_n^2)/Q_n^2$ and $2P_n/Q_n$ inflates to **$3L \approx \frac{3}{2} I_*$**, then collapses back to output depth $2L = I_*$ through the division gate cancelling the common factor $Q_n$ (cancellation wormhole); one round costs $13\alpha L$, cumulative action $13\alpha I_*$.

2. **Partial-fraction (Heron) reconstructed context $k_{\text{Heron}}(\square) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\square + \frac{2}{\square}\right)$**:
* semantically $k_{\text{Heron}} = k_{\text{naive}}$, but because the projective poles $\{0, \infty\} \subset \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})$ are split over the rational field into two degree-one branches, only **one** $\Delta$ fan-out is needed.
* in the last doubling round ($n = N/2 \to N$), the two juxtaposed wires on cross-section $[2]$ are $x_{N/2} = \frac{P_{N/2}}{Q_{N/2}}$ and $\frac{2}{x_{N/2}} = \frac{2 Q_{N/2}}{P_{N/2}}$ (both reduced, since $P_m$ is always odd). Their instantaneous total flux is:

$$\Sigma_{\text{Heron}} = \ln P_{N/2} + \ln(2 Q_{N/2}) = \left(\frac{N}{2}h_0 - \ln 2\right) + \left(\frac{N}{2}h_0 - \frac{1}{2}\ln 2\right) + o(1) = I_* - \frac{1}{2}\ln 2 < I_*$$

* the two branches are combined by the addition gate directly into $\frac{P_{N/2}^2 + 2Q_{N/2}^2}{P_{N/2} Q_{N/2}}$ (**zero common-factor cancellation waste**); one round costs only $7\alpha L$, and the cumulative action is compressed to **$7\alpha I_* + O(\epsilon_0 \log N)$**.

> **Instance check ($N=8$)**: $x_4 = \frac{17}{12}$, $x_8 = \frac{577}{408}$, endpoint depth $I(x_8) = \ln 577 \approx 6.3578$. Inside the Heron handle of $x_4 \mapsto x_8$, the widest cross-section is $(\frac{17}{12}, \frac{24}{17})$, with instantaneous total flux $\ln 17 + \ln 24 = \ln 408 \approx 6.0113 < \ln 577$.

**Geometric conclusion**: two algebraically equivalent nonlinear contexts have strictly unequal worldsheet measures in the HTWR core ($\Sigma_{\text{naive}} = \frac{3}{2}I_* > \Sigma_{\text{Heron}} = I_*$). With no extra channel constraint, the total-flux bound $\Sigma_{\max} = I_*$ already suffices to accommodate the Heron doubling chain, producing no quadratic time degradation.

## III. Orthogonal decomposition of the cutting interface: longitudinal wavefront depth vs transverse catalytic flux

The above analysis exposes a masking effect of the total flux $\Sigma(\mathbf{u})$: because the main carrier wire must reach depth $I_*$ at the endpoint, handles opened at early shallow layers ($L \ll I_*$) all hide inside the "spatial shadow" cast by the endpoint fat tube. To separate the "longitudinal target depth inherent to the problem" from the "transverse workspace occupied by nonlinear fan-out", one must orthogonally decompose the spatial flux of any cutting cross-section $\mathbf{u} = (u_1, \dots, u_m) \in X^m$:

$$\Sigma(\mathbf{u}) = \underbrace{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}_{\text{longitudinal main wavefront depth}} + \underbrace{\Sigma_{\text{aux}}(\mathbf{u})}_{\text{transverse catalytic / entangling flux}}$$

where:

* **longitudinal main wavefront depth** $\Phi(\mathbf{u}) := \max_{1 \le j \le m} I(u_j)$: measures how far the deepest wire on the current cross-section has advanced toward the hyperbolic boundary cusp (the state coordinate evolving from $0$ to $I_*$);
* **transverse catalytic flux** $\Sigma_{\text{aux}}(\mathbf{u}) := \Sigma(\mathbf{u}) - \Phi(\mathbf{u}) = \sum_{j \neq j_{\max}} I(u_j)$: measures the **total auxiliary entangling tube diameter** derived from the fan-out singularity $\Delta$ and simultaneously suspended on the cross-section, other than the main carrier wire.
* the pure-tree single-wire geodesic flow ($m=1,\ g=0$) always has $\Sigma_{\text{aux}} \equiv 0$;
* the Heron full doubling chain needs $\Sigma_{\text{aux}} = I(x_{N/2}) = \frac{1}{2} I_* + O(1)$ before the last round closes.

Taking the transverse catalytic quota $\Sigma_{\text{aux}} \le \sigma_{\max}$ as the spatial constraint variable, the universal spacetime tradeoff law of the HTWR core then appears in full.

## IV. The universal spacetime uncertainty lower bound (holds for any target and any DAG)

### Theorem 1 (HTWR spacetime action–catalytic-flux uncertainty principle)

Let the target state $y^* \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})$ have information height $I_* = I(y^*) > \ln 2$. For **any** nonlinear string-diagram network $h \in \mathrm{Hom}([0], [1])$ in the HTWR core preparing $y^*$, if it admits a leaf-slicing sequence (topological evaluation order) such that the transverse catalytic flux of every spatial cross-section $\mathcal{C}_t$ satisfies $\Sigma_{\text{aux}}(\mathcal{C}_t) \le \sigma_{\max}$ ($\sigma_{\max} \ge 0$), then the total action of $h$ obeys the unconditional rigid lower bound:

$$\mathcal{A}(h) \ge \frac{\alpha (I_* - \ln 2)^2}{2(\sigma_{\max} + \ln 2)}$$

#### Rigorous proof

Decompose the string diagram $h$ in topological order into $M$ single-generator slices $g_1, g_2, \dots, g_M$; since shared subgraphs are already represented explicitly as $\Delta$ generators, each gate occurs exactly once in the sequence, and $\mathcal{A}(h) = \sum_{t=1}^M c_{g_t}$. Let the longitudinal main depth on cross-section $\mathcal{C}_t$ be $\Phi_t := \Phi(\mathcal{C}_t)$, with initial $\Phi_0 = 0$ and final $\Phi_M = I_*$. Consider all possible generators $g_t$ whose single-step increment $\Delta \Phi_t := \Phi_t - \Phi_{t-1} > 0$:

1. **constant injection, erasure $\epsilon$, and fan-out $\Delta$**: none produces a new state exceeding the maximal height of the original cross-section (for $\Delta(u) \mapsto (u,u)$ the maximum is unchanged; only the copy is added to $\Sigma_{\text{aux}}$), hence $\Delta \Phi_t \le 0$.
2. **unary Möbius generator $\gamma \in \{z \mapsto -1/z,\ z \mapsto z \pm 1\}$**: inversion strictly preserves height $I(-1/z) = I(z)$; adding or subtracting $1$ satisfies $\max(\vert{}p \pm q\vert{}, \vert{}q\vert{}) \le 2\max(\vert{}p\vert{}, \vert{}q\vert{})$, so $I(z \pm 1) \le I(z) + \ln 2$. If $\Delta \Phi_t > 0$, then necessarily $\Phi_t = I(z \pm 1) \le I(z) + \ln 2$, whence:

$$\Delta \Phi_t \le \ln 2 \le \sigma_{\max} + \ln 2, \qquad c_{g_t}(z) \ge \alpha I(z) \ge \alpha(\Phi_t - \ln 2)$$

3. **binary scattering generator $\star \in \{+, -, \times, \div\}$**: let the input wires be $u_1, u_2 \in \mathcal{C}_{t-1}$ with $I(u_1) \ge I(u_2)$. Since $u_1, u_2$ occupy two independent slots on cross-section $\mathcal{C}_{t-1}$, the thinner wire $u_2$ is necessarily counted in the transverse catalytic flux, i.e.:

$$I(u_2) \le \Sigma_{\text{aux}}(\mathcal{C}_{t-1}) \le \sigma_{\max}$$

By the triangle inequality of the logarithmic Weil height (multiplication/division satisfy $I(u_1 \star u_2) \le I(u_1) + I(u_2)$; addition/subtraction satisfy $I(u_1 \pm u_2) \le I(u_1) + I(u_2) + \ln 2$), the output state $u_{\text{out}} = u_1 \star u_2$ satisfies $I(u_{\text{out}}) \le I(u_1) + I(u_2) + \ln 2$. If $\Delta \Phi_t > 0$, then $\Phi_t = I(u_{\text{out}})$ and $\Phi_{t-1} \ge I(u_1)$, giving at once the two core inequalities:

$$\Delta \Phi_t \le I(u_{\text{out}}) - I(u_1) \le I(u_2) + \ln 2 \le \sigma_{\max} + \ln 2$$

$$c_{g_t}(u_1, u_2) \ge \alpha\big(I(u_1) + I(u_2)\big) \ge \alpha\big(I(u_{\text{out}}) - \ln 2\big) = \alpha(\Phi_t - \ln 2)$$

Extract from the sequence $\{\Phi_t\}_{t=0}^M$ the strictly increasing subsequence $0 = \hat{\Phi}_0 < \hat{\Phi}_1 < \dots < \hat{\Phi}_R = I_*$ that refreshes the running maximum, and write the step lengths $\delta_r := \hat{\Phi}_r - \hat{\Phi}_{r-1} \in (0,\ \sigma_{\max} + \ln 2]$. Using $\frac{\delta_r}{\sigma_{\max} + \ln 2} \le 1$ and the monotonicity of right-endpoint Riemann sums, summing over all record steps with $\hat{\Phi}_r > \ln 2$:

$$\mathcal{A}(h) \ge \sum_{\hat{\Phi}_r > \ln 2} \alpha(\hat{\Phi}_r - \ln 2) \ge \frac{\alpha}{\sigma_{\max} + \ln 2} \sum_{\hat{\Phi}_r > \ln 2} (\hat{\Phi}_r - \ln 2)\,\delta_r \ge \frac{\alpha}{\sigma_{\max} + \ln 2} \int_{\ln 2}^{I_*} (x - \ln 2)\,dx = \frac{\alpha(I_* - \ln 2)^2}{2(\sigma_{\max} + \ln 2)}$$

This completes the proof. $\square$

## V. Catalytic-wormhole big-step jumping and the tight Pareto-frontier theorem

Theorem 1 gives the hyperbolic inverse bound $\Omega(I_*^2 / \sigma_{\max})$. Below, for the Pell convergent $x_N$ (for **any integer $N \ge 1$**, without assuming $N = 2^K$), we construct an elegant "linear main pipeline + resident catalytic wormhole" network proving that this lower bound is **tight up to a constant** over the whole interval $\sigma_{\max} \in [0, \frac{1}{2}I_*]$!

### Core construction: the zero-fan-out catalytic stepping identity of the main wire

If one fan-outs $\Delta$ the main wire $x_n$ directly at depth $L \gg \sigma_{\max}$, the transverse flux instantly spikes to $L > \sigma_{\max}$, violating the constraint. To circumvent this prohibition, use $(1+\sqrt{2})^{n+k} = (P_n + Q_n\sqrt{2})(P_k + Q_k\sqrt{2})$ and the Pell identity $P_k^2 - 2Q_k^2 = (-1)^k$ to rewrite the jump map of span $k$ as a **degree-one fractional-linear (Möbius) decomposition** in the main variable $x_n$:

$$x_{n+k} = \frac{x_n x_k + 2}{x_n + x_k} = x_k + \frac{2 - x_k^2}{x_n + x_k} = x_k + \frac{(-1)^{k+1} / Q_k^2}{x_n + x_k}$$

**The geometric miracle**: in this expression the heavy main variable $x_n$ occurs **only once (strictly linear passage, zero $\Delta$ fan-out!)**. Given the catalytic step parameter $k \ge 1$ (with catalytic primitive depth $s := I(x_k) \approx k h_0$), one only needs to keep a pair of lightweight catalyst wires $(x_k,\ c_k)$ resident in the transverse direction, where $c_k := 2 - x_k^2 = \frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{Q_k^2}$ (depth $I(c_k) = 2\ln Q_k \approx 2s$):

1. **remainder pre-elimination kills the divisibility sawtooth**: for any $N$, do division with remainder $N = M k + r$ ($0 \le r < k$). At the start, use the Heron doubling chain to prepare the initial main state $z^{(0)} = x_r$ (or start from $x_k$ and skip one round if $r=0$) together with the catalyst pair $(x_k, c_k)$. Since $r < k$, the peak flux of the preparation does not exceed $3s$, and the action cost is only $O(\alpha s)$.
2. **Just-in-time four-gate wormhole crossing**: in the $j$-th big-step crossing ($z = x_{r+jk} \mapsto x_{r+(j+1)k}$, main depth $L_j \approx (r+jk)h_0$), execute in order:
* fan out one copy of the catalyst $x_k$ and add it to $z$: $z_1 = z + x_k = \frac{Q_{r+(j+1)k}}{Q_{r+jk} Q_k}$ (main depth rises to $L_j + s$, instantaneous transverse flux $4s$);
* invert the main wire: $z_2 = 1 / z_1$ (main depth $L_j + s$, transverse flux $3s$);
* fan out one copy of the catalyst $c_k$ and multiply $z_2$ by it: $z_3 = z_2 \cdot c_k = \frac{(-1)^{k+1} Q_{r+jk}}{Q_k Q_{r+(j+1)k}}$ (one factor $Q_k$ cancels automatically; main depth $L_j + 2s$; **global peak transverse flux is $s + 2s + 2s = 5s$**);
* fan out one copy of the catalyst $x_k$ and add it to $z_3$: $z_{\text{next}} = z_3 + x_k = \frac{Q_k P_{r+(j+1)k}}{Q_k Q_{r+(j+1)k}} = x_{r+(j+1)k}$ (the cancellation wormhole eliminates the residual factor $Q_k$; main depth snaps back precisely to $L_{j+1} = L_j + s$!).

> **Arithmetic instance check ($n=3 \to 5,\ k=2$)**: main state $x_3 = \frac{7}{5}$, catalysts $x_2 = \frac{3}{2},\ c_2 = 2 - \frac{9}{4} = -\frac{1}{4}$. The four steps evolve as $z_1 = \frac{7}{5} + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{29}{10} \longrightarrow z_2 = \frac{10}{29} \longrightarrow z_3 = \frac{10}{29}\times\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = -\frac{5}{58} \longrightarrow z_4 = -\frac{5}{58} + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{82}{58} = \frac{41}{29} = x_5$; the two rounds of wormhole cancellation of $Q_2 = 2$ fit together seamlessly.

One crossing round consumes total action $\Delta \mathcal{A}_j = \alpha(4 L_j + 12s) + O(\epsilon_0)$, with the transverse flux strictly bounded by $5s$ throughout. Take $s = \frac{1}{5}\sigma_{\max}$ (i.e. step length $k = \lfloor \frac{\sigma_{\max}}{5 h_0} \rfloor$), and sum over $M \approx \frac{I_*}{s} = \frac{5 I_*}{\sigma_{\max}}$ rounds:

$$\mathcal{A}_{\text{BSGS}}(\sigma_{\max}) = \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} 4\alpha (j s) + O(\alpha M s) = 2\alpha s M^2 + O(\alpha I_*) = \frac{10\alpha I_*^2}{\sigma_{\max}} + O(\alpha I_*)$$

$$\frac{\alpha (I_* - \ln 2)^2}{2(\sigma_{\max} + \ln 2)} \;\le\; \mathcal{A}^*(\sigma_{\max}) \;\le\; \begin{cases} \dfrac{3\alpha}{2 h_0} I_*^2 + O(\alpha I_*), & 0 \le \sigma_{\max} < 5 h_0 \\ \dfrac{10\alpha I_*^2}{\sigma_{\max}} + O(\alpha I_*), & 5 h_0 \le \sigma_{\max} < \dfrac{1}{2} I_* \end{cases}$$
