## 1. The object: the thrice-punctured sphere $\mathbb H^2/\Gamma(2)$

Let $M=\mathbb H^2/\Gamma(2)$ with $\Gamma(2)$ the level-2 principal congruence subgroup

$$\Gamma(2)=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb Z): a,d \text{ odd},\ b,c \text{ even}\Big\}/\{\pm I\},$$

freely generated by the two parabolics (classical)

$$A:z\mapsto z+2,\qquad B:z\mapsto \frac{z}{2z+1},$$

with three cusps $\{0,1,\infty\}$; the quotient is the thrice-punctured sphere (cusped pair of pants, genus 0, torsion-free). Basepoint $\tilde p=i$.

The front $\Sigma_t=\{x:d(\tilde p,x)=t\}$ is a hyperbolic circle (Euclidean circle center $i\cosh t$, radius $\sinh t$); the quotient distance circle $C_t=\pi(\Sigma_t)$ twists as $t$ grows (script `048` emits `048-fire-front-twist.gif`, 70 frames, $t\in[0.15,1.7]$; left = circles colliding in $\mathbb H$, right = the folded surface).

## 2. General result: event time = half displacement, $N(t)$ = orbit count (`PROVED`)

The same argument as the flat torus (021 §2), with lattice vectors replaced by deck elements:

1. $C_t$ self-intersects at $q$ iff some $\gamma\neq e$ has two circle points $x,\gamma x$ both at distance $t$ from $\tilde p$, i.e. $x\in\Sigma_t(\tilde p)\cap\Sigma_t(\gamma^{-1}\tilde p)$;
2. these two hyperbolic circles meet iff $\|\gamma\|:=d(\tilde p,\gamma\tilde p)<2t$, so each crossing is born at the **event time** $t=\tfrac12\|\gamma\|$;
3. each unoriented class $\{\gamma,\gamma^{-1}\}$ gives two transverse crossings (the two intersection points), collapsing to one self-tangency at $t=\tfrac12\|\gamma\|$.

Hence

$$N(t)=\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma(2)\setminus\{e\}:d(\tilde p,\gamma\tilde p)<2t\},$$

the **orbital counting function** — the hyperbolic generalization of the flat-torus $N(t)=r_2(2t)-1$ (Gauss circle problem), with $\mathbb Z^2$ lattice lengths replaced by $\Gamma(2)$ hyperbolic displacements. Event times = half the based geodesic-loop length spectrum of $(M,p)$.

## 3. Computation on $\Gamma(2)$ (`COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED EXAMPLE`)

Generator displacements $\|A\|=\|B\|=\operatorname{arcosh}3\approx1.7627$, so the **first event** is $t=\tfrac12\operatorname{arcosh}3\approx0.8814$ ($+4$; two unoriented classes $\{A\},\{B\}$, two chords each). Event table (script printout, ordered by $\|\gamma\|$):

| $t=\|\gamma\|/2$ | $\gamma$ (unoriented representatives) | $N(t)$ jump |
|---|---|---|
| $0.8814$ | $A,\ B$ | $0\to4$ |
| $1.4436$ | $AA,\ BB,\ Ab,\ Ba$ | $4\to12$ |
| $1.7627$ | $AB,\ BA$ | $12\to16$ |
| $1.8184$ | $AAA,\ BBB,\ AbA,\ BaB$ | $16\to24$ |
| … | … | … |

Samples: $N(0.5)=0$, $N(0.89)=4$, $N(1.5)=12$, $N(1.8)=16$, $N(2.1)=32$. Self-checks: `len(crossings_at(t))==N_exact(t)`, and each crossing's two preimages $\{z,\gamma z\}$ lie on $\Sigma_t$ ($d(\tilde p,z)=d(\tilde p,\gamma z)=t$) — all pass.

**Completeness (stated honestly)**: reduced words are enumerated to depth $12$ (72 words kept with $\|\gamma\|\le5.0$); depth $11$ vs $12$ are **STABLE** on the spectrum $\|\gamma\|<4.4$ (40 words agree). But for a **cusped** group, $A^k$ has displacement $\sim2\log k$ (parabolic directions grow logarithmically), so the naive word-depth completeness test is unusable; rigorous completeness needs a horoball refinement. Recorded here as an **unresolved point**, not silently asserted.

## 4. Gauss words (`COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED EXAMPLE`)

| $t$ | $N(t)$ | Gauss word (cyclic) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.95 | 4 | `1 1 0 3 2 2 3 0` |
| 1.25 | 4 | `1 1 0 3 2 2 3 0` |
| 1.60 | 12 | `5 1 1 5 4 10 11 0 3 11 10 7 6 2 2 6 7 9 8 3 0 8 9 4` |

($t=0.95$ and $t=1.25$ share the same word: no crossing is born between events, so the Gauss word is constant on each inter-event interval; the adjacent `1 1`, `2 2` are "young" crossings born at $t=0.8814$ whose two angular positions are still close.)

## 5. Growth: exponential rate = critical exponent $\delta=1$ (`STANDARD CONSEQUENCE` + `COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED`)

Orbit counts: $R=2\to4$, $R=3\to12$, $R=4\to24$, $R=4.4\to40$; the ratio $\#/e^R\approx0.5$ is roughly constant, consistent with $\#\{\gamma:\|\gamma\|\le R\}\sim C\,e^{\delta R}$ ($\delta=1$) for a finite-area cusped Fuchsian group (Patterson/Margulis). Thus $N(t)\sim C\,e^{2t}$ (rate $\approx2\delta=2$). Contrast with the flat torus $N(t)\sim4\pi t^2$: **polynomial on the flat torus, exponential on the hyperbolic thrice-punctured sphere** — the quantitative form of "negative curvature makes the twist pattern complicate exponentially fast".

## 6. Interface to the SC square / $\{4,\infty\}$ (`STRUCTURAL PROPOSAL`, not yet implemented)

The SC square $\mathcal Q_e$ has deck group $\Gamma_4=\langle J,T\rangle\cong C_2*C_4$ (Note 019) **with torsion** (order-2 and order-4 elliptics → cone points). Then:
- the torsion-free bijection "crossing ↔ unoriented deck element" needs correction for elliptic elements ($\gamma^n=e$, cone-point loops);
- cone points force cusp/reflection singularities in the front (no longer purely transverse self-intersections).

So the SC square needs its elliptic/cone-point contributions separated before reusing the §2 skeleton. **Left as the next step.**
