\begin{abstract}
Paper~0 constructs arithmetic expression spaces, the arithmetic motions on
them, ripple geometry, the accumulative commutative space, and the contact
structure of arithmetic tearing.  The present paper develops the layer that
those constructions presuppose: the syntax of process.  We classify the finite
binary expression trees whose internal operations have a unique legal
evaluation order; they are exactly the trees whose internal vertices form a
single spine.  Marking an accumulator turns such a tree into a chronological
word of one-hole contexts, with operand-slot chirality kept distinct from
temporal reversal and from functional inversion.  Non-degenerate bilateral
contexts act projectively on $\PP^1(K)$ and generate $\PGL_2(K)$ over an
arbitrary field; those fixing infinity form the affine, or Borel, sector, and
the same proof gives the rank-one Bruhat decomposition.  A three-letter
sublanguage over $\Q(\sqrt2)$ realizes the $q=4$ Hecke group and yields an
explicit marked history that is projectively trivial while being ordinarily
inadmissible, which is why ordinary admissibility is kept separate from
projective non-degeneracy.  The paper then develops the zero theory of regular
arithmetic expression spaces: the zero locus is a smooth curve; a connected
complete boundaryless regular space splits as $Z(a)\times\R$; branching zero
networks are therefore excluded in the regular category; and a smooth parameter
family with spatially regular zeros has a submersive total zero set, subject to
a stated properness warning.  A singular arithmetic expression space is defined,
and one isolated-zero model is verified.
\end{abstract}
