\begin{abstract}
Arithmetic expressions with a single internal spine admit two elementary
extreme forms.  In a left-expanded expression the already-computed subtree
occupies the first operand slot, whereas in a right-expanded expression it
occupies the second.  Both have a unique internal evaluation order, but their
natural geometric readings differ.  Left expansion produces a covariant point
path on the addition--multiplication grid of the basic upper-half-plane model.
Right expansion, especially through division by the evolving value, produces
fractional linear maps; pulling back standard horocycles gives a nested pencil
of circles tangent at the operator pole, which we call a ripple pencil.

We develop these pictures in parallel.  Arithmetic reciprocation exchanges a
finite point with infinity, geometric-series recursion realizes
$1/(1-r)$ as the fixed-point closure of $S_{n+1}=1+rS_n$, and the singular
parameter $r=1$ is exactly where the finite fixed point escapes to the
projective point at infinity.  Repeated right reciprocal expansion gives the
usual continued-fraction recursion $z\mapsto a+b/z$, whose fixed points govern
convergence when an attracting branch exists.  Finally, matrices place point
paths, poles, fixed points, and ripple pencils in one projective geometry:
left-expanded affine arithmetic stabilizes infinity, while right-slot division
introduces inversion and, together with translations and nonzero scalings,
generates $\PGL_2(K)$.  The paper is an elementary prelude to the marked
bilateral histories, affine flow, torsion, and contact geometry developed in
Paper~I.
\end{abstract}
